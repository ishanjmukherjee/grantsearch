Does This Look Okay, Mom?Mother-Daughter Interactions with Body Image
Amy FanWeinberg College of Arts and Sciences Northwestern University
Introduction
Negative body image in women has been a prevalent issue throughout history. Whether women are expected to be voluminous or thin depending on the era, societal expectations have left many women feeling insecure and dissatisfied. Current research has suggested that oftentimes negative body image is passed down from mother to daughter (Hillard et al., 2016). While there is a variety of research on mother-
daughter interactions with body image, there isn’t a lot of
research that specifically studies the connotation of words used by mothers and how that may correlate with satisfaction with appearance and body. As views on weight and criticism may differ, another area worthy of examining is how mother-daughter interactions differ across cultures (Haudekm& Rorty& Henker, 1999).

Main Research Question: What is the relationship between the connotation of
adjectives mothers use to describe their daughters’ appearances and daughters’
satisfaction with appearance?
Primary Hypothesis1)There may be a negative correlation between number of negative words used by
mothers to describe daughters and daughters’ satisfaction with their overall
appearance.Secondary Hypotheses1)
There may be a positive correlation between mothers’ body satisfaction reported by daughters and daughters’ own body satisfaction.
2)Asian or Asian American women may agree more with the statement that it is
common in their culture for mothers to critique their daughters’ physical
appearance than other races and ethnicities.
