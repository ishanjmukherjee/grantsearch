This fall, the Northwestern Interdisciplinary Law Review (NILR) enters its fifth year of publication. The Review's first four volumes have established the journal as a consistent and accessible source of interdisciplinary legal scholarship, drawing from fields such as history, literature, economics, art theory, and philosophy. Working alongside talented law students, practitioners, and professors, consecutive classes of Northwestern University students have carved out a space for substantial legal dialogue between scholars of various disciplines and perspectives. The current editorial board feels privileged to carry on this tradition and remains committed to featuring the same high-quality literature that readers have come to expect from the NILR.

Each year, we read hundreds of submissions to fill our annual spring volume. Ideal articles will exemplify sound scholarship, make original arguments, and contribute something substantial to the existing interdisciplinary study of law. In addition, we ask authors to temper excessive academic rhetoric, which will allow a diverse audience to engage in intellectual discussion and debate.

We would like to especially thank Diana Snyder and the very helpful staff of the Buffett Center for International and Comparative Studies; our publisher, the Law Review Press; and Associate Provost Ronald Braeutigam and Dean Mary Finn, whose generous financial support have made this project possible.

For an idea of our editorial perspective, please refer to our website for previous volumes. We invite your submissions and questions at nilr@u.northwestern.edu.

Alison Gifford and Patricia Radkowski
Editors-in-Chief, 2011-2012
