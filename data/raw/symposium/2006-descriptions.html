DANIEL ABBE


Reading in Borges and Benjamin


Faculty: Peter Fenves


Jorge Luis Borges and Walter Benjamin have come to be seen as two of the most important writers of the 20th century: Borges in fiction and Benjamin in the realm of literary criticism. Two questions immediately present themselves for a study of the two: how and why should they be read together? As facile as it may seem, I suggest that reading their own techniques of reading is a useful way to consider them. Benjamin is in almost every respect a more serious writer than Borges. The urgency of his own historical situation—1930s Germany—is palpable in his writings, which are motivated by a consideration of history that brings the present into a crisis. Borges’ literary works, on the other hand, do not respond to any sort of critical situation. They are rather a product of his reading: he dedicated his life to reading (and re-reading) as much as possible. Looking at reading in the two writers thus puts Borges on equal footing with Benjamin. I focus a reading of Benjamin on texts where he submits books and libraries to his critical procedure. What emerges from these writings is that Benjamin’s conception of reading is not all that dissimilar from Borges’. Each writer, for example, is interested by the possibilities of collecting books, and the fate of each particular book in a collection. Their reading techniques, in fact, illuminate each other’s writings: an understanding of Borges and Benjamin in this way has not yet been attempted.




JODI ANDERSON


The Role of Race in Public Opinion of American Governmental Policies


Faculty: Daniel Molden


Are Americans’ political ideologies and endorsements of government policies toward African-Americans influenced by racism and prejudice? This question is a constant fixture in American policy discourse. While numerous sociological, political, and psychological theories debate this problem, there is still the question of whether or not racial prejudice drives individual’s attitudes and government policy preferences. A new field has recently developed that applies subconscious measurements (implicit attitudes) and these measurements have shown to be a predictor of individual’s behavior towards members of the group s/he has implicit bias against (Dovidio, Kawakami, & Gaertner 2002; Fazio et al. 1995; Greenwald et al. 1998; Payne et al. 2005). Applying regression analyses, my research examines leading theories in the fields of sociology, political-sociology, psychology, and two forms of implicit attitude measurement (Implicit Attitude Test, Affect Misattribution Procedure), along with surveys addressing current government policy, to determine whether or not there were any relationships between or among the variables. These different variables have never been tested together to determine interactions or correlates. Results indicated that the theory of Symbolic Racism, above and beyond any other variable, most aptly described the trends of resistance of white Americans’ public opinion regarding government policies directed towards African Americans.

YANETH BELLO


Sana, Sana Colita de Rana:

A Generational Study of Contemporary Counterborder Curanderismo


Faculty: Monica Russel y Rodriguez


This project offers a generational perspectives of curanderismo, the Mexican system of traditional healing which addresses illnesses of the body, mind, and spirit, in a Mexican American family in Chicago and contemporary counterborder curanderismo. Here counterborder makes reference to the spatially fluid and ever changing state of curanderismo. More importantly, it addresses the lack of a static, solid, or distinct border between the practice of curanderismo in Mexico and in Chicago. A counterborder perspective is important in the study of contemporary curanderismo whose practical day to day use is rooted in the ‘traditional’ form but has developed into an ‘updated’ version for a newer community in a newer setting.

Ethnographic fieldwork included interviews and participant observation and took place in Apipilulco and Iguala, Guerrero, Mexico and throughout the city of Chicago. Data was divided into the generational groups studied in order to aid in the analysis. As a result, this generational study invites the visualization of the tradition through an artistic medium to incite the study of culture through contemporary art. Through video and installation, audiences are exposed to curanderismo and build a personal relationship to it. Moreover, they share the artist’s role as insider/outsider, colonized/colonizer, and academic/artist. The inclusion of contemporary art also facilitates the discussion of a contemporary counterborder curanderismo through the medium of the visual arts.




ADRIENNE BERNHARD


Paganism and Pessimism in the Novels of Thomas Hardy


Faculty: Jennifer Brody


To what can we attribute Thomas Hardy's misery, his disheartened assessment of man, and, as one scholar of Hardy, Ruth Firor has remarked, his "brooding pity for all living things"? Traditional scholarship stresses the tragic vision of Hardy's works: the author's profound melancholy and grim resignation fascinate readers as subjects for intellectual inquiry. If we come to understand Hardy through his personal memoranda as well as his novels, we can better explain the vast implication of his religious philosophy against the backdrop of Victorian England. My research explores the degree and manner in which Hardy extracts a religious sensibility from ancient ritual, and whether that sensibility allows his characters to live and die with integrity. I argue that Hardy documents the survival of an older, Pagan era that had its basis in dependence on the earth, not on god; his novels explore Paganism as an alternative to the dogma of institutional Christianity. For the project, I studied relevant texts at Oxford University's Bodleian Library and visited Dorset (the birthplace of Hardy) and the Dorset County Museum, where many of Hardy's original manuscripts are held. This spring, I also worked at the Beinecke Library at Yale University and examined Hardy's private collection of novels and essays.




PAULINA BLACKINTON


Traditional Ecological Knowledge, Acculturation, 

and Child Health Among the Tsimane’ of Lowland Bolivia


Faculty: Thomas McDade


The project examines the associations between traditional ecological knowledge (TEK), acculturation, and child health in an indigenous population in the Bolivian Amazon. Traditional ecological knowledge, the knowledge of wild plant species and their uses, is universal to indigenous cultures; recent studies are revealing its significance for health, as well as other issues. The effect of acculturation on health is still ambiguous, as is the effect of acculturation on TEK. The research population, the Tsimane’, was relatively isolated until the 1950s, but for past several decades has become increasingly integrated into a market economy. However, the Tsimane’ (who number about 8,000 and live in over 100 villages) have acculturated at different paces and in different ways; additionally, their location in an area noted for its botanical diversity make the Tsimane’ good subjects for this study. Previous work has examined the distribution of TEK among the Tsimane’ and the culturally relative value of specific plants, but TEK has not yet been studied in relation to child health. Results indicate that traditional ecological knowledge is beneficial for child health, especially for malnourished children. The impact of acculturation on health is small but generally negative; TEK is negatively associated with parental acculturation. This study highlights the importance of TEK for child health as well as the threat to TEK from acculturation. Future policies should recognize the importance of TEK and try to protect it from degradation.




MEGAN BLACKWELDER


The Cultural Double Standard: 

Understanding Perceptions of Infanticide in Nineteenth-Century Great Britain and India


Faculty: Ken Alder


In the early nineteenth century, infanticide remained prevalent in Great Britain. The typical accused woman was a young, poor single mother. Though the official penalty for infanticide was the death penalty, juries were often reluctant to convict; many were excused on the grounds of temporary insanity. In the same period, officials of British East India Company and English missionaries were soon enacting invasive policies to prevent the practice in India. The British took an avid interest in controlling infanticide because it provided a way to establish local authority in areas otherwise outside their political control by disrupting marriage patterns that perpetuated hierarchies among the Indian elite. The British colonials were horrified by female infanticide practices of the elite castes, which they attributed to exorbitantly high dowry costs.

How could the British adopt an indifferent attitude toward infanticide in Britain, continuing to acquit accused mothers in Great Britain (without acknowledging the circumstances leading to her crime) while at the same time strive to suppress the practice in India? The historian Satadru Sen argues that the British seized upon the moral opprobrium attracted to female infanticide to further their colonial ambitions in India, and my research expands upon this argument to compare British colonial perceptions of Indian infanticide with the prevalence of infanticide among British mothers. I suggest that infanticide became entirely embedded in the British cultural double standard of the implicit tolerance of the practice among poor women destroying their illegitimate children in Great Britain, but condemning the practice of the elite married couple murdering their female children in India.




SAM BLINSTEIN


Synthesis and Characterization of Silica Aerogel for Use In Superfluid 3He Experiments


Faculty: William Halperin


Silica aerogels are a form of highly porous glass (~98% porosity) that can be used to introduce impurities into superfluid 3He. It has been speculated that new superfluid phases of 3He might be stabilized inside of anisotropic silica aerogels. To this end, we established a facility at Northwestern to produce both isotropic and anisotropic aerogels for use in the study of superfluid 3He. We found that highly isotropic aerogels can be chemically synthesized by a process that includes solvent exchange followed by a slow and even temperature ramp during the supercritical removal of this solvent. Anisotropy can then be introduced into these samples via compression. In addition, we have found that samples that shrink during the synthesis process exhibit intrinsic anisotropy. We have measured the degree of anisotropy in our samples using Small Angle X-ray Scattering (SAXS). These characterized samples are now being used to study the effect of anisotropy on the phases of superfluid 3He. The next goal of our group is to produce even higher porosity (≥ 99%) aerogels to explore how the 3He system transitions from the pure to the impure state.




GREGORY BOK


Floating-Point Emulation for Fault Tolerance and Reliability


Faculty: Russ Joseph


The decreasing size of transistors found in modern microprocessors has led to an increasing number of chips lost to manufacturing defects and in-operation failures, resulting in increased cost due to failed parts and a reduction in processor lifetime. A common technique for mitigating these effects is duplication of selected hardware, providing a layer of redundancy in the event of a failure. While this has proven to be effective for hardware components with regular structures, such as caches, it cannot be applied to components such as arithmetic logic units (ALUs), which consist of a heterogeneous set of smaller components. To provide redundancy for these components, the entire unit must be duplicated, which can be very inefficient. An alternative solution is to utilize existing hardware to emulate the instructions that are dependent upon a failed resource. Floating-point arithmetic operations, which require a separate ALU, are particularly well suited for emulation, as they can be readily represented as sequence of integer arithmetic operations, which do not utilize floating-point hardware. The implementation presented here uses a virtual machine monitor (VMM) to detect failures. When a failure is detected, execution of instructions affected by the failure causes the processor to execute the stored emulation routines, computing the correct result without requiring the use of the hardware affected by the failure. In all, instruction emulation can be used to make microprocessors more tolerant of both in-operation faults and manufacturing defects, which translates into longer device lifetime and a potential reduction in cost.

STEPHANIE BRODY


From Faithfulness to Factuality: Semantic Change in the English Vocabulary of Truth


Faculty: Brady Clark


The semantic change of English words relating to truth form a cognitive vocabulary varying between the moral and the epistemic. While true originally referred to one’s faithfulness and loyalty, true now also defines a statement or belief that is factual, consistent with reality. A corpus study documenting this change across the Old through Early Modern English periods yields an explanatory account based on: 1) mechanisms of semantic change and 2) motivations for the change provided by a social model of language contact. The semantic change of true words demonstrates two hypothesized trends: a pattern from deontic to epistemic meaning as well as a correlating rise of subjectivity (Traugott 1989). The original faithfulness meaning of true corresponds with deontic meanings of obligation, while the later meanings of factuality invite a more epistemic sense of knowledge and beliefs. The Invited Inferencing Theory (Traugott and Dasher 2002) models this change, as speakers draw on pragmatic meanings that get negotiated through conventionalizing implicatures and thus spark change. An examination of the dates and frequencies of this particular semantic change shows the likelihood for French influence as a contributor towards that process. Indeed, English and French contact was a mixture of primarily borrowing with minimal shift-induced interference. Contact with French provided new lexical choices (such as loyal and faithful) for describing the original sense of true. Combining models of contact with acquisition, the anti-synonymy constraint (in which new words mean new things) helps explain why increased use of the French words corresponded with the increasing use of the new meanings for true.




ROBIN BRUSEN and PARMEDE VAKIL


Empirical Evidence for Existence of 3D-Photonic Nanojet


Faculty: Vadim Backman


The diffraction limit of light restricts the use of the visible spectrum to image sub nanometer sized samples. Current methods for imaging sub-diffractional objects include Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), and Scanning Tunneling Microscopy (STM), but these instruments are often incompatible and highly tedious in their application to many biological samples. However, recent FDTD simulations have shown that dielectric spheres on the order of the wavelength of the electromagnetic spectrum are capable of producing a region of high intensity radiation on the sphere’s shadow side: a phenomenon called the 3D-Photonic Nanojet. Furthermore, simulations have shown that placing an object within this high-intensity region produces a significant enhancement in backscattering through the sphere. One application of this technology is imaging sub-diffractional samples using visible light. Until recently, the existence of the 3D-photonic Nanojet has been purely theoretical. Over the past several months, we have employed various chemical and optical techniques to observe empirically this phenomenon. These techniques include microsphere coupling using biotin-streptavidin protein-protein interactions and more recently, nanofabrication, using Quanta SEM.




CAITLIN YOSHIKO BUYSSE


Conversion and Islamic Mysticism in The Autobiography of Malcolm X


Faculty: Souleymane Bachir Diagne


Malcolm X, one of America’s “most outspoken revolutionaries,” is famous for his sharp rhetoric, leadership in the Black Revolution, and dedication to civil and human rights. Most scholarship on Malcolm X addresses his political and racial ideas; the sparse religious scholarship examines his leadership in the Black Muslim movement and his outward demonstration of religiosity through a sectarian lens. The project takes a bold step away from the existing literature by approaching The Autobiography of Malcolm X through a lens of his conversion to Islam. Research focused on Malcolm’s two prominent spiritual experiences: his conversion in prison and his pilgrimage to Mecca (Hajj). While these events are typically considered two separate conversions (to the Nation of Islam and Sunni Islam), my analysis asserts their fundamental continuity. The prison conversion can be likened to the spiritual experience of withdrawal, retreat from worldly distraction into pure contemplation of God. Malcolm’s conversion in prison echoes the Prophet Muhammad’s withdrawal into Cave Hira, where he received the First Revelations (Qur’an). Years later, the Hajj witnessed the culmination of Malcolm’s conversion. In gathering, Malcolm experienced the unity of creation (brotherhood of man) as a reflection of the unity of God. Despite the apparent diversity of creation, there is an underlying unity that reflects the unified nature of its Creator. The deepest significance of Malcolm’s withdrawal and gathering, then, attests to the same reality: the unity of God. Prison and Hajj therefore represent two faces of Malcolm’s singular conversion experience.

GABRIELA CANTARERO


Brainstem Responses and Their Relationship to Cortical Encoding of Speech Sounds


Faculty: Nina Kraus


Previous studies have shown an abundance of speech-evoked brainstem and cortical deficits in learning disabled (LD) children, but with much less attention given to the relationship between them. In this study, we investigated whether speech-evoked brainstem timing delays, evident in roughly 30% of LD children predict cortical response abnormalities to speech syllables. Our hypothesis is that speech encoding proceeds from peripheral to central neural structures and that deficits at lower levels (i.e. the brainstem) influence higher level responses (i.e. the cortex). The LD children were further divided into 2 subgroups: LDd (LD with delayed brainstem timing) and LDn (LD with normal brainstem timing). Preliminary results using point to point t-test scores across group grand average waveforms of normal learners (NL), LDd, and LDn children have shown that children with a delayed brainstem timing may also have poor cortical timing (characterized by a reduced P1/N1b slope). Additionally, as a group, only LDn have shown a different representation of /da/ vs. /ga/ stimuli. In conclusion, these findings suggest that delayed brainstem timing is related to an overall cortical encoding deficit, regardless of any difference between the speech stimuli /da/ and /ga/. Also, that LDn have cortical encoding deficits that are unique from those observed in their LDd counterparts.




VALERIE M. CASEY


Preparation of a Graphite NanocompositeUsing Ring-Opening Metathesis Polymerization


Faculty: SonBinh T. Nguyen


In recent years, polymer nanocomposites have attracted a great deal of interests as a new generation of strong, lightweight materials that may revolutionize the transportation, construction, and sport industries in the same manner that polymers ushered in the plastic age in the mid 1900’s. Critical to the enhanced mechanical properties of these nanocomposites are the particle shape and size, the surface characteristics, and the degree of dispersion of the selected nanofiller components inside polymer matrices. Given these criteria, graphene nanoplatelets, the structural unit of graphite and single wall carbon nanotubes, are predicted to be excellent nanofiller components due to their remarkable in-plane mechanical, electrical, and thermal properties. In an effort to create a homogenous graphite-polymer nanocomposite, we attempted to covalently grow monodisperse polymer chains from both graphite oxide (GO) and thermally expanded graphite oxide (TEGO) using ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP).

Ammonium-salt-based precursors to ROMP initiators were synthesized and successfully intercalated between layers of GO as evidenced by IR spectroscopy, X-ray diffraction, and elemental analysis. We also attempted to attach Grubbs 1st-generation ROMP catalyst to these intercalated initiator precursors. However, subsequent ROMP experiments failed to yield polymer nanocomposites, most likely because Grubbs catalyst, a relatively bulky molecule, could not intercalate between the GO layers. TEGO was then substituted as source of graphene nanoplatelets since the distance between each graphene layer in this material was greatly increased in comparison to GO, allowing for incorporation of Grubbs catalyst. A pyrene-based ROMP initiator precursor was first bound to the TEGO surface through van der Waals interactions. Subsequent attachment of Grubbs 1st-generation ROMP catalyst to the pyrene derivative was then attempted followed by ROMP reaction with cis-cyclooctene. While monodisperse polymers could be formed readily, they are cleaved from the TEGO surface quite easily, most likely because the bonds between the pyrene initiator and the TEGO surface were weak, and no nanocomposites could be isolated.




MARINA CHERNOV


14-month-olds’ interpretations of nouns and adjectives


Faculty: Sandra Waxman


Language development research is fundamental to our understanding of how infants perceive the world and, more specifically, how they establish relations between words and objects. Infants gradually begin to make connections between words and their referents during their first year. Distinct words, such as nouns and adjectives, can highlight distinct aspects of the same object, such as the category to which it belongs (e.g., animal, vehicle, dog) and the properties that it possesses (e.g., big, fast, red), respectively. Previous research has shown that 14-month-old infants correctly map novel nouns specifically to categories of objects, whereas they have more general expectations about adjectives, mapping them to both categories and properties of objects. This data was based on an interactive task (where an experimenter presents the materials). We have now developed a new automated task (presented as a video) to assess whether the same results can be attained using a comparable procedure. We predicted that 14-month-olds who participated in the new procedure would reveal the same results as in the interactive procedure. Three experiments were developed with the automated procedure to test this prediction. Results revealed that 14-month-old infants do in fact reflect the same behavior as they did in the previously conducted study (Experiments 1 and 2). Moreover, results showed that 18-month-olds who participated in the automated procedure maintain the notion that nouns refer to object categories, and have begun to map adjectives specifically to properties of objects (Experiment 3).




VINA CHHAYA


Drug Toxicity and Adverse Side Effects in Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):

Effect of Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) on Gastrointestinal Flora


Faculty: Jim Radosevich


Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), such as Protonix, are given to patients to counter the effects of gastroesophageal reflux disease by shutting down proton pumps, thereby increasing the pH of the stomach. It is known that certain strains of bacteria colonizing the oral cavity and upper gastrointestinal tract have proton pumps similar in structure to human proton pumps. This DNA homology suggests that PPIs may also affect the activity of prokaryotic proton pumps. For this experiment, nineteen strains of Lactobacilli were inoculated in microtiter plates in media at a pH of 4.5, 5.0, or 5.5, depending on the sensitivity of the bacteria, with two-fold dilutions of Protonix at a range of 2.5 mg/mL to 2.5 μg/mL. Bacterial growth was monitored and the minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) of the drug was determined. In cases where growth was inhibited, Gram-staining was carried out to visualize any potential morphological changes in the bacteria. Furthermore, growth curves were obtained to determine if growth was impeded at pharmacological doses of Protonix. Eight of the nineteen Lactobacilli strains were found to have an MIC below 313 µg/mL. Additionally, Gram-staining of certain strains, such as L. s. salivarius 11741, revealed conformational changes in the bacteria grown in the presence of Protonix. Growth curves at pharmacological doses demonstrated inhibited growth of certain strains. Thus, the slowed growth and conformational changes observed in a number of strains in the presence of Protonix suggest that taking PPIs could cause a disturbance in the normal balance of flora found in the oral cavity and gastrointestinal tract, possibly leading to the long-term complications seen in some patients.

SHAWN CROWLEY


Confounding Logic: Peronist Disapproval and Support of Carlos Menem in Argentina


Faculty: Benjamin Ross Schneider and Karen Alter


Argentine President Carlos Menem’s domination of the Peronist Party was a surprise to many. Menem’s implementation of free-market, neoliberal policies took the traditionally populist, union-based Peronist Party on an unprecedented rightward turn. Menem’s presidency was also marred by corruption scandals. Despite these developments and his deviation from the party line, however, Menem maintained the support of his party and was reelected. My thesis explains this phenomenon. 

Menem was successful for many reasons. His free-market reforms attracted the pro-business sector and the upper class, formerly anti-Peronist groups of voters. Menem’s economic reforms slashed Argentina’s soaring inflation rate and began to stabilize the economy, saving many from bankruptcy. The President forged strong alliances with party leaders from the provinces of Argentina, ensuring them their autonomy in exchange for their constituents’ support. Finally, he was also able to divide his opponents, keeping them from forming a successful coalition against him. Menem’s ultimate success, though, was premised on the weakly institutionalized nature of the Peronist Party. Menem’s ability to get away with corruption, the defeat of his opponents, his appeals to the upper-class and his alliance with provincial leaders were only possible in a party that lacked rigidity, clear rules and obvious channels for power. Peronist leaders were willing to overlook Menem’s corruption and anti-Peronist principals if their job-security, party funding and autonomy were assured. These leaders ensured the support of their constituents and Carlos Menem, an allegedly corrupt and in many ways anti-Peronista leader, was able to maintain his power for two terms.




SAMANTHA CRUZ


Production of Carbon Nanotube Films as Conductive Substrates for Cardiomyocyte Growth


Faculty: Mark Hersam


The research examined the production of optically transparent conductive substrates for in vitro studies of cardiac muscle cells. These muscle cells, cardiomyocytes, cause the heart to beat. Cardiomyocytes, when grown in vitro, lack intercellular electrical conduction, which leads to an asynchronous beating of heart cells. For future prospects of the implantation of these heart cells into the body, a biocompatible conductive substrate must be designed, which will enhance this cell communication. Carbon nanotube (NT) films are used for this purpose because of its intrinsic electrical conduction properties and apparent biocompatibility. Two methods, namely filtration and spin coating were used to produce these films. The filtration method is a reproducible means of creating uniform NT films, however the transfer of these films to transparent substrates for the observation of the heart cells was found to be tedious and difficult. A more direct way of creating the NT films on transparent substrates, spin coating was investigated, and methods of increasing throughput by parallel film production were designed. After production, the NT films were biofunctionalized using peptide amphiphiles (PA) in order to improve the biocompatibility with the cardiomyocytes. The films were surface characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), water contact angle measurements, and surface profilometry to investigate uniformity, biofunctionalization and thickness respectively.

SOHIER DANE and SARA PATRAWALA


Calumet Cluster Project Proposal


Faculty: Kimberly Gray


Natural resource damage has occurred in Indian Ridge Marsh (IRM), one of several marshlands adjacent to the Calumet Cluster Superfund site and a plan is needed for remediation and restoration IRM. The Calumet Cluster is a highly contaminated property on the South side of Chicago. Both hazardous and non-hazardous wastes were dumped, incinerated or stored here. Nothing on site prevents contaminated material from being transported by wind, surface, or groundwater to adjacent marshlands. Remediation activities for the site have been divided into three phrases: capping, groundwater control, and remediation. We are concerned with Phase III, which will assess and remediate areas surrounding the Cluster by claiming Natural Resource Damages under the Superfund Law. The party responsible for executing the remediation plans, as directed by the Superfund Law, is the Board of Trustees. The Board of Trustees is selected from government agencies such as US EPA, Illinois EPA, US Fish and Wildlife, among others. We will provide a priority list and remediation recommendations to the Board of Trustees to help guide the creation of the Natural Resource Damages Assessment (NRDA) and Restoration Plan for IRM. We will identify contaminants present and what can be done to remediate the marsh to “baseline conditions”. Once the site has been characterized, we hope to create a link between the contamination and the Cluster site, in order to identify the responsibilities of the Potentially Responsible Parties. We then will present our findings to the Board of Trustees, who will make the final decision for remediation plans for the site.




ALEC DAVIS


Light-weight Mirrors for Space-based Optics


Faculty: Semyon Vaynman


Our project deals with the development of lightweight optical mirrors for use in space. The main idea is to develop a way to manufacture metallic mirrors that are both lighter and tougher for space purposes by combining the metal surfaces with composite backings that make them significantly lighter and stronger. These lighter mirrors are much easier and cheaper to launch into space. We have developed three main methods that can be used to create these mirrors. All three methods begin with a composite substrate in the shape of a 2-inch diameter puck produced by the Cornerstone Research Group (CRG). In the first method, the puck supplied by CRG also came with a thin layer of gold applied by CRG. We electroplated a layer of nickel (approximately 100 microns thick) onto the gold surface of the puck. The gold was needed to conduct the electricity needed to electroplate the nickel onto the surface, as the composite does not conduct sufficiently itself. The nickel layer was then polished to produce the mirrored surface. The second method takes a different approach by first vacuum depositing a layer of gold onto a piece of glass. The composite puck is then glued to the gold layer using an epoxy, and then the puck and gold removed because the gold does not adhere well to the glass. The puck is then electroplated with nickel and the nickel layer polished. The third method uses a different composite substrate provided by CRG that is electrically conductive enough to be directly electroplated onto. In this method, the nickel is directly electroplated onto the surface of the composite and then polished.




JONATHAN DAVIS


High and Mighty:

A Cross-Cultural Look at the Phenomenon of Sanctioned Drug and Alcohol Use in War


Faculty: William Irons


There is a long and largely unacknowledged history of warriors and soldiers using drugs during battle, dating back to at least 2500 B.C.E. Substances ranging from natural opium and psychoactive mushrooms to rum and synthetic amphetamines have been utilized by cultures all over the world in wartime to improve performance by minimizing fear, increasing ferocity, and alleviating fatigue. Among the beneficiaries of this practice were the Berserk Vikings, the Incans, and the Zulu in their victories over the British. As a result of significant pharmacological advancements, however, the past hundred years have seen a significant increase in the use of drugs in war. Nearly every major combatant in World War II, including for Nazi Germany and the United States, distributed a combined total of amphetamine tablets in excess of 300 million to their pilots and soldiers. For the United States, use continues to have a significant role in military operations. This research highlights the pervasiveness of these practices and, by doing so, expands current frameworks of understanding both modern military pharmacology and war in general. The use of drugs and alcohol throughout history has proven beneficial to the physical effectiveness and mental survival of soldiers. Substances have managed the stresses and demands of war that have otherwise considerably limited individual performance. Despite the social stigma attached to drug use today, sanctioned drug and alcohol use in warfare remains an important tool in the 21st century.




PIYAPA DEJTRAKULWONG


Characterization of Seismicity in Alaska/Aleutian Subduction Zone


Faculty: Donna Jurdy


At subduction zones, the seismicity of the main thrust is clearly influenced by lateral heterogeneities such as asperities. By contrast, intermediate-depth seismicity is less well characterized and understood. Most studies have focused on across-slab seismicity variations such as double seismic zones. In this project, we identify clusters of intermediate depth seismicity in the Alaska/Aleutian subduction zones to reveal along-strike heterogeneities in the slab that may improve our understanding of stress sources and slab mechanics. We employed several cluster analysis techniques, the most successful being the Gath-Geva algorithm (GG), a fuzzy clustering technique, and the Gaussian mixture model (GMM). The two clearest clusters are identified by both methods. They are located near the Eastern end of Alaska subduction zone. The easternmost cluster corresponds to the segment C of Ratchkovski and Hansen (2002). This suggests that the cluster may be related to a material heterogeneity in the subducting plate or increased stress due to the differential motion of the neighboring slab segments. Three additional clusters may appear related to a possible tear in the subducting plate around longitude 180° possibly due to collision with the Bowers ridge. The anomalous focal mechanism of the 1906 Aleutian earthquake that occurred in that area is an additional evidence for this possible tear.




MELISSA M. DERR


“W” Is For Working Class:

How the Perception-Based Campaigns of George W. Bush

Courted the White Working Class in 2000 and 2004


Faculty: Kenneth Janda


With George W. Bush as its candidate, the Republican Party’s success among white working class voters was significantly greater in the 2000 and 2004 presidential elections than in the elections of 1992 and 1996. What accounts for this substantial defection? Defining “working class” as earning less than median income—based on responses to each election year’s American National Election Study survey—and having no more than a high school education, this project aims to explore the ways in which perception became everything to the Bush campaigns. Though the white working class (WMC) is no longer strongly affected by campaign appeals along economic class lines, I argue that WMC members are still motivated to vote along cultural class lines by their perception of campaign values in close elections. To this end, this project explores the effects of rhetoric, framing, trust building, priming and message consistency in the Bush campaigns. Spring boarding off previous studies, I maintain that, even though the white working class may not agree with all the issue platforms of the Republican Party, national data shows that Bush was still successful in painting himself as a “preferred” candidate through the values and style of his campaigns. Issue positions alone, then, played a muted role in determining voting behavior in 2000 and 2004. Studying the ways in which campaign rhetoric and image framing affects the white working class demographic over these four election years is important because, in close elections, this is a swing demographic that, according to some, accounted for a majority of Bush’s victory margins in 2000 and 2004.




MAGDALENA DUMIN


The Phenotypic Analysis of the Sleep/Wake Cycle in Neonatal Inbred Mouse Strains


Faculty: Fred Turek


The relationship between poor sleep and poor health underscores the need to identify the components of sleep that are genetically encoded. Studies in adult mice have revealed several sleep traits that are quantitatively driven by chromosomal elements. In the current study, we used a novel protocol to analyze sleep and wakefulness in pups from five inbred mouse strains. Pregnant females (14 days post-pregnancy) from each strain were housed in 14L:10D for the remainder of their gestational period. Food and water were available ad libitum. Faculty: J. P. Rosenfeld


When one perceives meaningful information, the electrical activity of the brain changes. This change is known in the field of psychophysiology as the P300 potential. Recent studies have shown that the P300 potential has important applications in deception detection. Prior to these studies, the only forensic test available to identify deception was the polygraph, which many experts believe to be inaccurate and easily beaten. During the polygraph test a criminal is presented with facts about a crime he/she is suspected of committing. If he/she recognizes these facts, a physiological change occurs, such as an increase in heart rate. Theoretically, the P300 lie detector works in the same way, but instead of using variables such as heart rate that can be easily manipulated by a perpetrator, the P300 potential is analyzed, leading to a more accurate decision. The experiment currently conducted measures the electroencephalogram (EEG) activity of Northwestern University students, (to date, sixteen experimental and nine control subjects have been run). Of the nine control subjects, only one showed a false positive response, thus, P300 was elicited without the presentation of relevant information. These results, showing significant individual detection, are promising and future research could extrapolate these findings to a real world forensic situation.

Faculty: David Uttal


Spatial ability is important in numerous domains including basic adaptation, reasoning, communication, and performance in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics) disciplines (Benbow, 1985; Delgado & Prieto, 2004; Baldwin, 1985).  Researchers have also demonstrated that spatial ability can improve with training (Baenninger & Newcombe 1989), schooling (Huttenlocher, Levine, & Vevea 1998), and even videogame playing (Subrahmanyam & Greenfield, 1994). Repercussion of this meta-analysis may include enhanced performance in STEM disciplines, more comprehensive and beneficial curriculum in school, and the generation of information relevant to the nature-nurture question.

The Time Course of Auditory Generalization


Faculty: Beverly Wright


Individuals can improve performance on many perceptual tasks with practice. This improvement is often accompanied by performance on untrained conditions, a phenomenon known as generalization. I investigated the time course over which learning generalizes to untrained conditions, using an auditory task on which listeners were asked to determine which of two time intervals between brief tones was longer (duration discrimination). I trained nine listeners on a single duration-discrimination condition for two days, and eight listeners on the same condition for four days. Before and after training, I tested all listeners on a variety of untrained duration-discrimination conditions to see if learning on the trained conditions generalized to any of them. The results showed that learning on untrained conditions did not occur until learning on the trained condition had begun. Listeners who were trained for two days improved only on the trained condition, while those trained for four days improved not only on the trained condition, but also on an untrained condition sharing the same duration. The finding that learning was initially specific to the trained condition and then broadened over the course of training implies that the neural circuit affected by training is initially stimulus-specific, and becomes less specific only after extended training. These results have implications for the design of perceptual training regimens, including those for the remediation of learning disabilities, since they suggest that maximal generalization may require continued practice on the trained condition even after some learning has occurred.
