# Filing projects under disciplines

You're helping build Prowl, a search engine over Northwestern undergraduate research: summer grants (SURG), Research Expo and Symposium posters, honors theses and journal papers, about 7,400 projects in every field from cell biology to art history. Its users are mostly students, searching by topic in their own words.

## What you're deciding

Prowl is adding a filter: a short row of disciplines a visitor can pick, one or several, to keep only the projects in them. Someone who types "water" and picks Earth & environment wants the hydrology and the policy, not the hydrogels.

Your job: for each project in your batch, say which of 37 disciplines it belongs in, the main one first.

## The list

These 37 are the only disciplines there are. Use the ids exactly as written. Each line says what has its home in the discipline.

Life sciences

- `biology` (Biology): living things at every scale. Molecular and cell biology, genetics and genomics, biochemistry, microbiology, developmental and structural biology, physiology, synthetic biology, plant biology, evolutionary biology, animal behavior.
- `neuroscience` (Neuroscience): the nervous system at any level, from molecules and circuits to brain imaging and behavior.

Health

- `medicine` (Medicine): disease, diagnosis and treatment. The clinical specialties (oncology, cardiology, dermatology, psychiatry, surgery and the rest), pharmacology, pathology, clinical and translational research, the health professions.
- `public-health` (Public health): the health of populations. Epidemiology, global health, health disparities, health services and systems, nutrition, health behavior and promotion, gerontology.
- `communication-disorders` (Communication sciences & disorders): speech, language and hearing science, audiology, speech-language pathology.

Physical sciences & math

- `chemistry` (Chemistry): all of it, organic to physical.
- `physics-astronomy` (Physics & astronomy): physics of every kind, astronomy, astrophysics, cosmology.
- `earth-environment` (Earth & environment): the planet and the living world on it. Geology, geochemistry, geophysics, planetary science, oceanography, physical geography; climate, environmental science and studies, sustainability; ecology and conservation; and the environmental side of another discipline (environmental policy, history, justice, engineering).
- `math-statistics` (Math & statistics): pure and applied mathematics, statistics, probability, biostatistics, data science.

Engineering & computing

- `biomedical-engineering` (Biomedical engineering): biomaterials, tissue engineering, medical devices and imaging, biomechanics, neural engineering, drug delivery.
- `materials-science` (Materials science): materials of every kind and scale, nanotechnology and nanoscience, polymers, metallurgy, electronic and energy materials.
- `chemical-engineering` (Chemical engineering): chemical and biological engineering, reaction and process engineering, separations, and the engineering side of synthetic biology.
- `engineering` (Mechanical, civil & electrical engineering): every other branch of engineering. Mechanics, fluids, robotics, aerospace, design and manufacturing; electronics, photonics, signal processing, computer engineering; structures, transportation, water and environmental engineering; industrial engineering and operations research.
- `computer-science` (Computer science): computing of every kind. Artificial intelligence and machine learning, human-computer interaction, systems, theory, graphics, and computing put to another discipline's questions when the computing is itself the work (computational biology, natural language processing).

Social sciences

- `psychology` (Psychology): all its branches: clinical, cognitive, developmental, social, personality, health. Also human development.
- `cognitive-linguistics` (Cognitive science & linguistics): two things. Linguistics: language as a system and in use, phonetics to pragmatics, sociolinguistics, psycholinguistics, language acquisition. And cognitive science, for work that sits across its member disciplines (music cognition, computational models of mind). Cognitive psychology, cognitive development and cognitive neuroscience aren't here: they are branches of psychology and neuroscience.
- `economics` (Economics): all its branches: labor, development, industrial organization, macroeconomics, behavioral economics, econometrics, game theory, public economics and public finance, financial economics.
- `business-finance` (Business & finance): finance, accounting, marketing, management, entrepreneurship, organizations.
- `political-science` (Political science): American and comparative politics, international relations, political theory, political behavior and institutions, human rights.
- `sociology` (Sociology): society, inequality and social life. Also criminology, demography, urban studies, migration, social movements, human geography, disability studies, science and technology studies.
- `anthropology` (Anthropology): cultural, medical, linguistic and biological anthropology, archaeology, ethnography.
- `education` (Education): teaching and learning, the learning sciences, schools and higher education, education policy.
- `law` (Law): legal studies, courts, constitutional and international law, criminal justice, law and society.
- `public-policy` (Public policy): the making and evaluation of policy, public administration, social policy, and the policy side of another discipline (health policy, education policy).

Humanities

- `history` (History): the past of any place and period, the ancient world included, and the history of a subject (economic history, history of science, legal history).
- `literature-languages` (Literature & languages): English and comparative literature, the literatures and languages of the world, ancient and modern (French, Spanish, Slavic, Latin, Asian languages and cultures), literary theory, cultural and postcolonial studies, translation, rhetoric.
- `philosophy` (Philosophy): all its branches, with ethics and bioethics, political philosophy, philosophy of science.
- `religion` (Religion): religious studies, theology, the study of particular traditions (Jewish studies, Islamic studies).
- `gender-sexuality` (Gender & sexuality): gender, women's and men's experience, and sexuality as the thing studied: gender studies, women's studies, sexuality and queer studies, feminist theory.
- `race-ethnicity` (Race & ethnicity): race and racism, and the identity and experience of a racial or ethnic group, wherever it lives: African American and African diaspora, Asian American, Latina and Latino, and Native American and Indigenous studies; diasporas and immigrant communities; critical race and ethnic studies.
- `area-studies` (Area & global studies): a region or country of the world, or the world as a whole, as the thing studied: African, Asian, Latin American, Middle East, European and American studies, international and global studies, international development.

Arts & media

- `art` (Art & art history): the visual arts, made and studied. Studio art, photography, graphic design, architecture; art history and criticism, visual culture, museum and curatorial studies.
- `theatre-performance` (Theatre, dance & performance): theatre, performance studies, dance, directing, acting, design for the stage.
- `music` (Music): performance, composition, musicology, ethnomusicology, music theory, music technology.
- `film-media` (Film & media): film and media studies, radio, television and film production, documentary, digital and interactive media, games.
- `creative-writing` (Creative writing): fiction, poetry, creative nonfiction, playwriting, screenwriting.
- `journalism-communication` (Journalism & communication): journalism, communication studies, political and health communication, advertising and public relations.

## How to file a project

**Ask who would look for it.** A student picks a discipline to see its work, and a researcher in it would recognize the project as their kind of work. File a project under each discipline where both hold. The two mistakes both cost. Leave a discipline out and the project vanishes for the people browsing it, with nothing on the page to say so. Add one that doesn't fit and that discipline's results fill with work that isn't its own.

**The first is the one the work is done in**: the discipline whose questions and methods it uses. A thesis that runs regressions on voter rolls is `political-science`; a play about a physicist is `theatre-performance` or `creative-writing`. Every project that says anything about itself has one.

**Add a second, rarely a third, only when the project really is a meeting of disciplines**, each of which would claim it: a health economics thesis is `economics` and `public-health`; a hydrogel for nerve repair is `biomedical-engineering` and `materials-science`; a wastewater treatment design is `engineering` and `earth-environment`; a study of protest songs in Chile is `music` and `area-studies`. Readers of this site's projects give about four in ten one discipline, half two, and fewer than one in ten three. Don't add a discipline:

- for being nearby. Neuroscience borders psychology and sociology borders anthropology; a project is in both only if it does both.
- for supplying a tool. Statistics in a psychology study, a microscope in a biology one, machine learning in a chemistry one, a survey in a public health one. `math-statistics` and `computer-science` are for work whose own contribution is the mathematics or the computing.
- for the topic alone. A study of how students learn biology is `education`, not `biology`. An economics thesis on hospital mergers is `economics`; it's also `public-health` only if health is what it's trying to understand.
- for the setting. See regions, below.
- for the motive. A solar cell is `materials-science`, not `earth-environment`, though clean energy is why it's being made. `earth-environment` is for work whose question is about the planet, the climate, ecosystems, pollution or sustainability.

**File the work, not the student or the department.** A project's sources say where it came from (a thesis's department, an advisor's department, a student's majors), and that's evidence of what it is, but an economics major can write a history thesis and a chemist can advise a materials project.

**Read everything you're given.** The abstract says most. Where there is none, the gloss is a reading of the title written for search, and may say it is a guess; file its best reading, with one discipline if that's all the title supports. The fields are how the project's search card named its fields, in its own words: a hint, often broader than the project.

Some lines the list draws:

- Life and health:
  - How living things work is `biology`, or `neuroscience` when it's the nervous system. That holds whatever the lab's department: bench work on the circadian clock from an endocrinology division is `biology`.
  - A disease, its diagnosis or its treatment as the thing studied adds `medicine`, or is `medicine` alone when the work is clinical: amyloid toxicity in Alzheimer's is `neuroscience` and `medicine`; a gene therapy for a skin disorder is `medicine` and `biology`; heart function measured in patients by imaging is `medicine`.
  - The health of populations, and health care as a system, is `public-health`.
  - Brain measures (fMRI, EEG, brain stimulation) used to study thought, perception or behavior in people are `neuroscience` and `psychology`.
  - Disorders of the mind studied as psychology (depression, anxiety, autism in a psychology lab) are `psychology`; add `medicine` only for clinical or psychiatric work.
- Regions and peoples:
  - `area-studies` is for work about a region or country outside the United States as such, its society, politics, history or culture: Tibetan identity in exile, growth in the transition economies of Eastern Europe, Liberia's peace process, how European Union members answer its social guidelines. That takes in the history and the politics of such a place, so a history of the French wine trade is `history` and `area-studies`. A country that is only where a general question was put doesn't add it: an HIV prevention trial in Uganda is `public-health`, and a test of auction theory on Chilean data is `economics`.
  - `race-ethnicity` is for work about race, or about a racial or ethnic group's identity and experience, anywhere: Nigerians in Japan, Samoan women's water practices, Black girls' accounts of school. A study that only reports results by race doesn't add it.
  - `gender-sexuality` is for work whose question is about gender or sexuality: the gender wage gap, women's activism, queer love poetry. A study that only reports results by sex doesn't add it.
- The history and criticism of an art goes with the art, not with `history`: musicology is `music`, film history is `film-media`, theatre history is `theatre-performance`, art history is `art`. The history of anything else is `history`, with its subject's discipline too when that discipline does such history itself (economic history, legal history).
- Classics has no line of its own: the ancient world is `history`, its texts `literature-languages`, its sites and objects `anthropology` (archaeology) or `art`.
- Making and studying: a play, film, story, composition or artwork the student made is filed under its art (`creative-writing`, `film-media`, `music`, `art`, `theatre-performance`). Add `race-ethnicity`, `gender-sexuality` or `area-studies` when that is what the work is about, since those are disciplines by subject: queer love poems by an Asian American poet are `creative-writing`, `race-ethnicity` and `gender-sexuality`. Add a research discipline (history, psychology) only if the project also does its research. A podcast or radio piece is `film-media`, or `journalism-communication` when it is reporting.
- Media studies is `film-media`; journalism and communication studies are `journalism-communication`; communication sciences and disorders is neither.
- A language studied as a system is `cognitive-linguistics`; a language's literature and culture are `literature-languages`.
- Policy: `public-policy` is for work that analyzes or evaluates a policy or program. A project about political argument over a policy is `political-science`.
- Finance is `business-finance`, with `economics` when it's done as economics (asset pricing, banking, corporate finance in an economics thesis).

## What you get for each project

Each line of your batch file is one project, as JSON:

- `id`
- `title`
- `sources`: each record of the project: what it is and its year, with an advisor's department and a student's majors where the source prints them.
- `abstract`: the project's abstract, or its first 1,500 characters; null when no source prints one.
- `glosses`: what each of the project's search cards says the project is. Empty for a project whose title gave its card nothing to go on.
- `fields`: the fields those cards name.

## Output

Write one JSON line per project to your output file, in input order:

`{"id": "...", "disciplines": ["...", ...]}`

- `disciplines`: one to three ids from the list, the main one first. `[]` only for a project that says nothing about what it is: a bare title that evokes nothing, with no abstract, gloss, department or major to go on.
- Add `"note": "..."`, a few words, only where you were torn. Say what pulled each way.

Judge each project yourself, by reading its line and using what you know. Don't script the judgments, call another model, or look anything up on the web; a script that only formats and checks your file is fine. Keep your verdicts in a draft file in your folder as you go, so an interruption loses little. Put any helper scripts in your own folder (given in your task), since other agents work alongside you. When you're done, check that the file has one valid line per project with ids in input order and that every discipline is an id from the list. Then report how many projects you filed under one discipline, two, three and none, plus up to ten you were least sure of and which way you went on each.
